# Content review

A running record of the doctrinal review of the site's text, in batches. Each batch does two things:
- **Fixes** clear errors in place.
- **Lists open points** for the author to decide. Mark each one *accept*, *reject* or *rewrite*, and the next batch carries it out.

The checks cover every number and list, every attribution (canonical or commentarial), every sutta citation and every reference to the *Manual*. Sutta references were checked against SuttaCentral where uncertain. The *Manual* itself was not to hand: references to it were checked against its known structure and the atlas's chapter map.

### Open: decided 2 November 2026, all accepted

The changes made: 1, softened to "hundreds of points". 2, "most of them". 3, Braun added to the sources. 4, kept. 5, the sources added. 6, replaced with "ferocity".

The points as they were raised:

1. **the-library: Ledi Sayadaw's "two hundred and forty-five points".** The figure is in the atlas and on Wikipedia, but I found no primary or scholarly confirmation. Erik Braun's *The Birth of Insight* (2013), chapter "The Great War of the Commentaries", is the place to check it. *Suggestion:* keep it, or soften it to "on hundreds of points" until it is checked.
2. **the-library: the Vibhaṅga "analyses each three ways".** True of most of its eighteen chapters, but not all have all three sections. *Suggestion:* change it to "analyses most of them three ways".
3. **the-library: Ledi "started a revival of lay Abhidhamma study that still continues".** This is a fair summary of Braun's argument, but it is a judgement, not a fact from the texts. *Suggestion:* keep it, and add Braun to the sources.
4. **the-library: the Tusita detail.** Accounts differ: some place Māyā in Tāvatiṃsa as the deva Santusita. The site now follows the commentarial version, Tusita. *Suggestion:* keep it.
5. **bases-and-elements: why three lists (aggregates for those confused about mind, bases for matter, elements for both).** This is commentarial, but no source is given. *Suggestion:* add "*Visuddhimagga* XV and the *Sammohavinodanī*" to the sources.
6. **a-chariot-of-parts: anger as "a hard, burning quality".** It is an image chosen to illustrate a dhamma, not a definition from the texts. The commentarial characteristic of hatred is "ferocity" (*caṇḍikka*). *Suggestion:* keep the image, or replace it with "ferocity, the same wherever it occurs".

### Checked and correct
These were checked and need no change:
- **Counts:** 89 or 121 cittas, and how the planes divide them: 54, 15, 12, 8. The 12 unwholesome cittas: 8, 2, 2. The 18 rootless cittas, 3 of them functional. The 24 beautiful sense-sphere cittas. Displeasure only in the two hate-rooted cittas. Bodily pain and pleasure only in body-consciousness.
- **Bases, elements, doors:** the 12 bases and 18 elements. The mind element as three cittas. The mental-object base: the 52 factors, 16 kinds of subtle matter, and Nibbāna. The 14 functions: 68 cittas with one each; the two equanimous investigating cittas with five; the 8 great resultants with four; the 9 sublime resultants with three. The doors: 46 cittas at the eye door, 67 at the mind door, 19 door-freed. The bases, including the immaterial planes, which have none.
- **Citations:** SN 5.10, SN 22.95, SN 35.23, MN 18, Ud 1.10, AN 2.25 and *Milindapañha* II.1.
- **History:** the seven books and what each does. The Third Council and Moggaliputta Tissa. Anuruddha's dates and nine chapters. Bodhi's 1993 revision of Nārada's translation.
