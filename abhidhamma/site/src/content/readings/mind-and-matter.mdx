The readings so far have taken the Abhidhamma's lists one at a time: the eighty-nine cittas, the fifty-two mental factors, the twenty-eight kinds of matter. Experience never comes in lists. Every moment of a life in this world is mind and matter at once, each holding the other up. The tradition calls the pair <Term>nāmarūpa</Term>, mind and matter, and this reading is about how they fit together. It is the hinge of the whole study: after it, the readings turn from *what there is* to *how it unfolds*.

## Two words, two depths

The word is older than the Abhidhamma. In the suttas, name-and-form is the fourth link of dependent origination, and the Buddha defines it there: "name" is feeling, perception, volition, contact and attention; "form" is the four great elements and the matter derived from them (SN 12.2). Consciousness is not part of it; it is the link just before, on which name-and-form depends.

The Abhidhamma keeps the pair but takes it down to its ultimate terms. Mind, <Term>nāma</Term>, is citta together with its mental factors; matter, <Term>rūpa</Term>, is the twenty-eight kinds. Together they are everything conditioned. The fourth ultimate reality, Nibbāna, is neither: it is not produced by anything and produces nothing, and only the path and fruition cittas take it as their object.<Note>The Dhammasaṅgaṇī, sorting all dhammas into the pair "nāma and rūpa", puts Nibbāna on the side of nāma, because, like mind, it is not matter and the mind bends towards it as an object. In the model here it stands apart, since it is not one of the two currents.</Note>

## Two currents that hold each other up

In the worlds that have both, mind cannot arise without a physical base, and much of the body is produced and kept going by mind. Each side feeds the other, and the arrows run both ways.

**Matter feeds mind** in two ways. Every citta rests on a <Term>vatthu</Term>, a base: the five sense organs for the five kinds of sense consciousness, and the heart-base for all the rest. And the five kinds of sense object, colour, sound, smell, taste and the tangible, are all matter.

**Mind feeds matter** in two ways too. Seventy-five of the eighty-nine cittas produce matter as they arise: posture, gesture, speech, a smile. And volition, which is kamma, produced the living core of this body from conception on: the sense organs, the heart-base, the life faculty.

Meanwhile heat and food keep renewing the body without asking the mind. The fire element in every group of matter gives rise to new groups, and the nutritive essence of food does the same.

Notice how much more traffic runs from mind to matter than the reverse. Matter helps mind mainly by being there first, as base and object. Mind helps matter in many ways at once, through its roots, its volition, its jhāna and path factors, its nutriments and faculties. And only once in a life do the two hold each other up as equals: at conception, when the rebirth-linking citta and the heart-base arise together, each the mutual support of the other.

## Where they meet: the six doors

Every kind of knowing needs an object, a door it comes through, and a base it rests on. Five of the six channels are matter from end to end; only the knowing is mind. The eye-door is the eye-sensitivity, its object is colour, its base is the same eye-sensitivity, and its consciousness is eye-consciousness. The mind-door is different: its door is a citta, the life-continuum, though the mind still rests on the heart-base. The six doors are drawn in <a href={u("readings/bases-and-elements/")}>The All, counted twice</a>.

## Matter in time

Mind and matter do not keep the same time. A citta lasts one mind-moment, with three sub-moments: arising, presence and dissolution. A unit of matter lasts seventeen mind-moments, fifty-one sub-moments. That is why a sense object that strikes a door lasts exactly long enough for one complete process, the seventeen moments traced in <a href={u("readings/the-mango-and-the-moment/")}>The mango and the moment</a>.

It also means that at any instant, dozens of generations of matter are alive in the body at once. In the model below, each bar is one unit of matter from its arising to its ceasing, in four lanes for its four origins; the row of discs is the stream of cittas.

<Scene3D name="loom" />

Kamma makes new matter at every sub-moment; a citta makes matter at its arising. Kamma-born matter stops arising seventeen mind-moments before death, so that it ends together with the death consciousness. Temperature-born matter alone outlasts death: the matter of a corpse is temperature-born.

## The smallest groups of matter

Matter never arises alone. It comes in small groups, <Term>kalāpa</Term>s, built on the same core: the four great elements and the four kinds of matter that always go with them, colour, smell, taste and nutritive essence. Kamma, mind, temperature and nutriment each add their own members. The model shows the twenty-one groups, with the eight inseparables at the corners of a cube.

The body, the Visuddhimagga says, is founded on earth, held together by water, maintained by fire and distended by air. Of the four, only water cannot be touched, which is why the tangible object is the other three: hardness or softness, heat or cold, pressure or motion.

## Inside the mind side

On the side of mind the unit is a citta with its factors. A factor arises with its citta, ceases with it, takes the same object and rests on the same base. The citta is the knowing; the factors colour it. Choose a citta to see which factors make it up.

## Mind makes matter

Not every citta can produce matter, and those that can do different things with it. The ten kinds of sense consciousness are too weak; the four immaterial resultants arise where there is no matter. A smaller set can move the body and the voice, and a smaller set again can make a smile.

## Not every world has both

Which bases a being has depends on its plane. In the immaterial worlds there is mind with no matter at all, and the citta needs no base. Among the non-percipient beings there is matter with no mind. Everywhere else the two currents run together, and the groups of matter present at the moment of rebirth-linking differ by plane.

## Why this matters

For practice, all of this comes down to one first step. The Visuddhimagga makes the discerning of mind and matter, <Term>nāmarūpa-pariccheda</Term>, the purification of view: the first knowledge of insight, on which the rest are built (see <a href={u("readings/the-tree-of-purification/")}>The tree of purification</a>). Seeing a moment of hearing as sound, ear and hearing, and nothing over and above them, is the beginning of seeing that there is no one behind the hearing. The model on this page is a map of what such seeing finds.
