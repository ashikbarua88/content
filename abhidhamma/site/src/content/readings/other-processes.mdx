The mango reading followed one process: a strong visible object at the eye door, seventeen moments, seven javanas. The same building blocks make every other process the Abhidhamma describes. Laid side by side, they show how much a single pattern can explain.

## At the mind door

A mind-door process, <Term>manodvāra-vīthi</Term>, is shorter. There is no sense organ to strike and no five-door adverting. The life-continuum vibrates and is cut off, mind-door adverting turns toward the object, and the javanas run. If the object is clear and the being is of the sense sphere, two moments of registration may follow.

The object can be anything: a thought, a memory, a concept, a past or future event. The man who ate the mango can remember it tomorrow in exactly this way.

## How a colour becomes a mango

A five-door process knows only a colour. Later teachers, Ledi Sayadaw among them, describe a series of mind-door processes that follow it: one that takes up the colour just past, one that grasps it as a whole, one that recognises its shape, one that names it.<Note>This series is a later teaching and not in the <i>Saṅgaha</i> itself. The <i>Manual</i>'s guide describes the first mind-door process that follows a five-door one; the rest is commentary on how recognition is built.</Note> Only then is there a mango, and only after that, an "I" who sees it.

## Absorption

The process of jhāna, absorption, begins like a mind-door process. Its object is a meditation sign. The first javanas are of the sense sphere and prepare the mind: preliminary work, access, conformity, and change-of-lineage. Then, at the fourth or fifth javana, the first citta of the fine-material sphere arises. In a first attainment it lasts one moment. In a practised meditator, absorption can run unbroken for hours.

## The path

The path process has the same shape. Insight has matured to the point of conformity, and the preparatory javanas are followed by change-of-lineage, which for the first time takes Nibbāna as its object. Then one moment of path, and two or three moments of fruition. Then the life-continuum resumes. The process is short, and it changes a person for good.

## Cessation, and the last process

Two more processes complete the set. In the attainment of cessation, possible only for non-returners and arahants who have mastered the jhānas, consciousness itself stops for up to seven days; it re-enters through a moment of fruition. And at the end of a life, a last process runs with weakened javanas, five instead of seven, and is followed by the death-consciousness.

## One pattern

What these processes share is a rhythm: a disturbance, a turning, a run of javanas, and a return to the life-continuum. Ordinary seeing, deep meditation and awakening are all made of it. The Abhidhamma's claim is not that awakening is ordinary, but that it happens to the same mind, in the same kind of moment, by the same laws.
