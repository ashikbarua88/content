The seventh chapter of the *Manual* is a compendium of lists, many of them inherited from the suttas: the taints, the floods, the bonds, the bodily ties, the clingings, the hindrances, the latent tendencies, the fetters, the defilements. Read as they come, they make a dense thicket of names. The Abhidhamma's contribution is to ask, of each item, which mental factor it really is.

The answer clears the thicket. Dozens of names come down to only fourteen factors, the unwholesome factors of the fifty-two.

## Many names, few factors

Take the four taints, <Term>āsava</Term>: sensual desire, attachment to existence, wrong views and ignorance. The first two are greed; the third is wrong view; the fourth is delusion. Three factors, four names. The four clingings, <Term>upādāna</Term>, are greed once and wrong view three times. The hindrances, which the <i>Manual</i> counts as six, are greed, hatred, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, doubt, and delusion as ignorance.

Count across all the lists and a pattern appears. Greed is in every one of the nine groups, under seventeen names. Wrong view is in eight groups, delusion in seven. A handful of factors does almost all the binding.<Note>The atlas counts by factor: each circle on the plate gives the number of items in a list that are, ultimately, that factor.</Note>

## Why keep the names?

If ten lists reduce to a few factors, why keep the lists? Because each name catches a different behaviour of the same factor. As a taint, greed seeps out through the senses. As a flood, it sweeps a being along. As a bond, it yokes them to existence. As clinging, it holds. As a latent tendency, <Term>anusaya</Term>, it lies dormant, waiting for a condition to arise.

The names are a field guide. The factor is the same animal; the names say where and how it shows itself.

## Categories that are conditions

The chapter's mixed categories hold a further surprise. Six of them are six of the twenty-four conditions. The six roots work as root condition. The jhāna factors work as jhāna condition. The path factors, the faculties, the predominants and the nutriments each have a condition of the same name. Only the nine powers have no condition of their own: they are the same factors as the faculties, seen as unshakeable by their opposites.

So the lists of chapter VII and the web of chapter VIII describe the same factors from two sides: what they are, and how they help.

## The requisites

The chapter ends with the thirty-seven requisites of enlightenment: the foundations of mindfulness, the right efforts, the bases of success, the faculties and powers, the factors of awakening, and the eightfold path. Reduced the same way, thirty-seven items are fourteen states. Energy appears nine times, mindfulness eight, wisdom five, concentration four, faith twice. The path, too, is a few qualities under many names, each name marking a stage or a use.
