The *Visuddhimagga*, Buddhaghosa's path of purification, pictures the path of insight as a tree. The things to be understood, the aggregates, bases, elements, faculties, truths and dependent origination, are the soil. Purification of virtue and purification of mind are the roots. The five purifications of wisdom are the trunk. At the crown is liberation.

The image is useful because it is organic. Nothing on the trunk can grow without the roots, and nothing at the crown without the trunk.

## Seven purifications

The scheme of seven purifications comes from a sutta in which Sāriputta and Puṇṇa compare the path to a relay of seven chariots: each takes the traveller only as far as the next, and the last arrives (MN 24). The commentaries make it the frame for insight.

**Purification of virtue** is restraint of body and speech. **Purification of mind** is concentration, access or absorption, the work of calm. These are the roots.

**Purification of view** is seeing mind and matter, <Term>nāma</Term> and rūpa, as they are, with no being or person behind them. **Purification by overcoming doubt** is seeing their conditions, in the present and in the past and future. **Purification by knowledge and vision of what is and is not the path** is the critical one: the meditator, seeing rise and fall, meets brilliant experiences, light, rapture, tranquillity, strong faith, and must recognise that attachment to them is not the path.<Note>The <i>Visuddhimagga</i> lists ten such "imperfections of insight". They are not bad in themselves; they are dangerous because they are so attractive that they stop the work.</Note>

**Purification by knowledge and vision of the way** is the run of insight knowledges that follows. **Purification by knowledge and vision** is the path itself.

## The knowledges

The *Manual* lists ten insight knowledges along the trunk. Comprehension sees dhammas in terms of the three marks, <Term>tilakkhaṇa</Term>. Knowledge of rise and fall sees each one arising and ceasing. Knowledge of dissolution sees only the ceasing. Then knowledges of fearfulness, of danger and of disenchantment: what dissolves so constantly cannot be a refuge. Then desire for deliverance, reflection, and equanimity toward formations, a balanced, unshakeable seeing of everything conditioned. Last comes conformity, which adapts the mind to the path.

## Three doors

Insight that is about to break through dwells on one mark more than the others, and that mark is the door it enters by. Dwelling on impermanence, it enters by the signless. Dwelling on suffering, by the desireless. Dwelling on not-self, by the void, emptiness. The doors differ; what lies beyond them does not.

## The crown

At the top of the tree a single process carries the change. After conformity comes change-of-lineage, <Term>gotrabhū</Term>, the first citta to take Nibbāna as its object while still belonging to the worldling. Then the path citta, <Term>magga</Term>, for one moment only, which cuts the fetters it is able to cut. Then two or three moments of fruition, <Term>phala</Term>, enjoying the same object. Then the life-continuum resumes, and the person is different for good.

Four paths cut ten fetters, <Term>saṃyojana</Term>. Stream-entry cuts three: identity view, doubt, and clinging to rules and rites. Once-returning weakens sensual desire and ill will; non-returning cuts them. Arahantship cuts the last five: desire for fine-material and immaterial existence, conceit, restlessness and ignorance.

One moment is enough because of what a path does. It does not add anything. It removes the conditions for certain cittas ever arising again, and from then on they simply do not come.
