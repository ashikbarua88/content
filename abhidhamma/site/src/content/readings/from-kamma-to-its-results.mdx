A volition ceases as soon as it arises, and yet it can ripen much later. The *Manual* traces two ways it does so. It can ripen as the first citta of a new life, the rebirth-linking consciousness. And it can ripen within a life, as the resultant cittas of seeing, hearing and the rest: the pleasant or unpleasant sights and sounds that come to a being without being chosen.

## Matching kamma to rebirth

The *Manual* matches each kind of kamma to the rebirths it can give. Twenty kinds of rebirth-linking cover all thirty-one planes.<Note>Nineteen kinds of citta can link a new life; the twentieth is the purely material rebirth of the beings without perception, who have no citta at all at rebirth.</Note>

Unwholesome kamma gives rebirth in the four woeful planes, by the unwholesome-resultant investigating citta. Weak wholesome kamma, two-rooted and done half-heartedly, gives a human or lower-spirit rebirth without roots: a life with serious disadvantages from the start. Middling wholesome kamma gives a human or divine rebirth with two roots, without understanding. Excellent wholesome kamma, three-rooted and done well, gives a three-rooted rebirth, with the capacity for jhāna and for the path in that very life.<Note>The <i>Manual</i> describes beings by these rebirth types: rootless, two-rooted, three-rooted. Only a three-rooted being can attain jhāna or the paths in that life.</Note>

## How the deed is done

What makes kamma weak, middling or excellent is not only what is done but how. The *Manual* weighs the roots of the citta, whether understanding was present, and the manner: whether the act was done gladly, with a clear mind before and after, or reluctantly and with regret. A gift given grudgingly and a gift given with joy and understanding are different kamma, though the gift is the same.

## Jhāna and the higher planes

The jhānas ripen in their own planes. In each of the first three levels, a jhāna developed weakly, moderately or fully leads to the lowest, middle or highest plane of that level. The highest jhāna leads to the plane of great fruit, or, with a turning away from perception, to the plane of beings without perception; the Pure Abodes receive only non-returners. The formless attainments likewise lead to the formless planes. This is weighty kamma: if a jhāna is maintained to the end of life, it takes precedence over everything else.

## Results within a life

Within a life, results arrive constantly. Unwholesome kamma ripens as the seven unwholesome-resultant cittas: unpleasant sights, sounds, smells, tastes, the pain of body-consciousness, and the receiving and investigating of such objects. One unwholesome citta, the restlessness citta, gives results in life but can never produce a rebirth.

The practical lesson the tradition draws is about urgency. The kind of rebirth decides what is possible in the life that follows: only a three-rooted being can attain the path in that life. Each life is the field prepared by the last, and each moment of javana is preparing the next.
