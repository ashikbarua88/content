The map of the eighty-nine sorted cittas by what they are. The third chapter of the *Manual* sorts them by what they do. It is the bridge from the map to the moments of a process: before you can follow a sight through seventeen moments, you need to know which cittas can fill each slot.

## Fourteen jobs

The *Manual* counts fourteen functions, <Term>kicca</Term>. Three happen outside any process: rebirth-linking, the life-continuum, and death. The rest are the stations of a process: adverting; seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting and touching; receiving; investigating; determining; javana; and registration.

Most cittas have one job only. Sixty-eight of the eighty-nine do just one thing: seeing only sees; a greed-rooted citta only ever runs as javana. A few are versatile. The two equanimous investigating cittas each do five jobs: they investigate and register in ordinary processes, and in beings with a rootless rebirth they also serve as rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death.<Note>The eight great resultant cittas do four jobs each: registration, rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death for human beings and gods of the sense sphere. The nine fine-material and immaterial resultants do three.</Note>

So a single kind of citta can open a life, carry it through every pause, and close it.

## Six doors

A <Term>dvāra</Term>, door, is where a process meets its object. Five are the sensitive matter of the senses. The sixth, the mind door, is the life-continuum itself, at the moment it is disturbed by an object.

Forty-six kinds of citta can arise through the eye door; sixty-seven through the mind door. Nineteen are "door-freed": the cittas of rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death, which arise outside any process and through no door at all.

## Six bases

Every citta in a being with a body stands on a physical base, a <Term>vatthu</Term>. The ten sense consciousnesses stand on their own sense organ. Every other citta stands on the heart-base. In the immaterial planes there is no matter and so no base; cittas there arise without one.

The base is not the same as the door. Seeing stands on the eye as its base, and the eye is also its door. But the javanas of that process arise through the eye door while standing on the heart-base.

## Why this chapter matters

Read as a table, the chapter is dry. Read as a description of a mind, it says something precise about freedom. Of the fourteen functions, only one, javana, makes kamma. Every other function is either a result of past kamma or merely functional. A process has seventeen moments, a life has millions of processes, and in all of it the room for doing something new is a single station, seven moments long.
