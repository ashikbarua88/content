"When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises." The suttas state the principle in a sentence, and then apply it as a chain of twelve links: <Term>paṭicca-samuppāda</Term>, dependent origination. It explains how suffering arises and continues, and how it ends.

The twelve are these. Ignorance conditions formations. Formations condition consciousness. Consciousness conditions mentality-materiality, which conditions the six sense bases. The bases condition contact, contact conditions feeling, feeling conditions craving. Craving conditions clinging, clinging conditions becoming, becoming conditions birth, and birth conditions ageing and death, with sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair.

## Three lives

The Abhidhamma's commentaries read the twelve across three lives.<Note>This is the commentarial reading, systematised in the <i>Visuddhimagga</i> and the <i>Manual</i>. The suttas themselves give several variants, and some modern teachers read the chain within a single life or a single moment. The Vibhaṅga itself, in its Abhidhamma section, applies the whole series to one moment of citta.</Note> <Term>Avijjā</Term>, ignorance, and the formations it conditions belong to the past: they are the kamma of a previous life. Consciousness, mentality-materiality, the six bases, contact and feeling belong to the present as results: the rebirth-linking consciousness and the life it begins. Craving, clinging and becoming belong to the present as new kamma. Birth and ageing-and-death belong to the future.

So the twelve links describe two turns of a wheel: past causes producing present results, and present causes producing future results.

## Twenty modes

The commentaries then notice something about this arrangement. Wherever ignorance and formations are present, craving and clinging are present too, since one does not occur without the others. And wherever kamma-becoming is, ignorance and formations are. So each set of causes is really five, and each set of results is really five.

That gives twenty "modes": five past causes, five present results, five present causes, five future results. The twelve links are a sketch; the twenty modes fill it in.

## Three rounds, two roots

The links can also be sorted into three rounds, <Term>vaṭṭa</Term>. The round of defilements is ignorance, craving and clinging. The round of kamma is formations and kamma-becoming. The round of results is everything else. Defilements give rise to kamma, kamma gives rise to results, and results, met with ignorance and craving, give rise to new defilements.

And the wheel has two roots. Ignorance is the root traced back from the past; craving, <Term>taṇhā</Term>, is the root traced from the present into the future. Neither is a first cause. Ignorance itself is said to be conditioned, by the taints, so the wheel has no beginning that can be found.

## Where it can be stopped

A wheel with no beginning sounds hopeless. The teaching is the opposite. A wheel of conditions is held together only by its links, and any one link that fails stops the whole.

The tradition points to the joint between feeling and craving. Feeling is a result: as long as there is a body and a mind, there will be pleasant, painful and neutral feeling. Craving is not a result. It is new kamma, arising in the javanas, and it can fail to arise. Where feeling is seen clearly, as feeling, craving loses its condition.

That is why the twelve links are taught at all. They are not a cosmology to be admired. They are a diagram of a machine, drawn so that you can see where to put your hand.
