A man sees a ripe mango and wants it. That moment has run through these readings since the chariot. Here it is read once more, lens by lens, to see the whole system at work on one small event.

**Two truths.** Conventionally, "I see a mango and want it". Ultimately: cittas, mental factors, an eye-base and a visible form, with no owner behind them. Both statements are true. The second is what the rest of the lenses examine.

**Cittas.** Six kinds of citta run in one process: five-door adverting, eye-consciousness, receiving, investigating, determining, and the javana, which in this case is greed with joy, unprompted, without wrong view. The registration cittas that follow are a seventh kind. Each has its number on the map of the eighty-nine.

**Factors.** In each javana, the seven universals and the six occasionals are present, with the four unwholesome factors that come with every unwholesome citta, and greed. Conceit may join them; wrong view does not, in this citta. None of the beautiful factors is present.

**Matter.** The eye's sensitivity and the colour of the mango are matter, each in its group of eight or more. The colour's group lasts seventeen mind-moments, exactly the length of the process. If the man reaches for the fruit, the javanas produce mind-born matter: the hand moves because a citta willed it.

**The process.** Three moments of the life-continuum, five that receive the object, seven javanas, two of registration: the seventeen moments of a strong object at the eye door. Only the javanas make kamma.

**Conditions.** The mango helps the seeing as object. The eye helps it as support and as a prenascent base. Each citta helps the next by proximity. The factors of each citta help one another by conascence and mutuality. Past habits of liking mangoes help the javanas by decisive support. And each javana helps the next by repetition.<Note>That is six of the twenty-four conditions in one glance, and a fuller reading finds more. The <i>Manual</i>'s synthesis still holds: object, decisive support, kamma and presence cover them all.</Note>

**Dependent origination.** Contact, feeling, craving: three links of the wheel in a single process. The <i>Vibhaṅga</i> teaches the whole series within one moment of citta, and the mango moment shows why that is not a forced reading. The feeling of the sight is a result; the wanting is new.

**Kamma.** The volition in the greed-rooted javanas is unwholesome kamma. It is small, and it is reserve kamma, unlikely to ripen at death unless repeated until it becomes habitual. Repeated, it becomes a habit, and that habit is a decisive support for the next sight of a mango.

**Planes.** The cittas are all of the sense sphere. A Brahmā of the fine-material worlds has eyes and could see the mango, but has no nose or tongue to smell or taste it. A being of the immaterial worlds, with no body at all, would not have this process. The man's plane sets what his mind can meet.

**The path.** The same seventeen moments are where practice works. Seeing is given. At the turn of attention, wise attention could have led to wholesome javanas: seeing the colour as colour, the wanting as wanting. In an arahant the javanas would be functional, merely seeing, and leave no result.

## What the lenses share

Laid side by side, the ten readings agree on a few things. Every one of them describes the moment completely in its own terms. None of them needs a self to finish the account. And every one of them points to the same small place, the javanas, as where something new is done.

That is what the Abhidhamma is, seen whole: not ten theories, but ten ways of looking at the same ground, each sharpening the others. The lists exist so that a single moment, like this one, can be seen clearly enough to be let go of.
