The mango has been seen and wanted through most of these readings. Here it is seen twice. In the first moment the man wants it. In the second, a friend is hungry, and he gives it away.

Contrast is how the categories become clear. Set the two processes side by side, moment by moment, and look for where they differ.

## The same, up to determining

For eight moments there is no difference at all. The same mango strikes the same eye. The life-continuum vibrates and is cut off in the same way. Five-door adverting turns toward it; eye-consciousness sees it; receiving, investigating and determining do their work. Every one of these is a result or merely functional. None of them is a choice, and none of them is different.

Seeing is never the problem.

## Where they part

At the javanas the two processes part. In the first, the javanas are rooted in greed: <Term>lobha</Term> with joy, the four unwholesome universals with it, delusion hiding what the mango is. In the second, they are rooted in non-greed and non-hatred, perhaps with understanding: generosity with joy, faith, mindfulness, and the whole set of beautiful factors.

The feeling can be the same in both: joy. That is one of the lessons of the contrast. Joy goes with opposite qualities. The roots decide what the moment is.<Note>The atlas's table runs both processes through every lens of Plate 14, row by row, and marks where they differ. Most rows change only from the javanas on.</Note>

## Everything downstream

From the javanas on, everything differs. The kamma made is unwholesome in one case and wholesome in the other. What it conditions is different: one strengthens a habit of wanting, the other a habit of giving. The link from feeling to craving is made in one process and not in the other. Even the matter produced differs: the hand that reaches to take and the hand that reaches to give are moved by different cittas.

## What the contrast teaches

Three things stand out. Seeing is the same in both; the first eight moments are given. Feeling is the same in both; joy does not tell you which kind of moment you are in. And kamma is made only where the two processes differ, in seven moments of javana.

That is the whole practical teaching of the Abhidhamma in miniature. It does not ask you to stop seeing mangoes, or to stop enjoying them. It points to the seven moments where the difference is made, and asks you to watch them.
