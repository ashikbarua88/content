question: Jhāna suppresses the hindrances but does not uproot them. What is it good for on the way out?
answer: >-
  It gives a mind unified enough to see clearly, and a happiness that needs no sense object. It is a basis for
  insight. Only the knowledge of the path uproots.
---
Calm (<i>samatha</i>) is the training of one-pointedness (<i>ekaggatā</i>) on a meditation subject until the hindrances are suppressed and the mind enters absorption (<i>appanā</i>) as jhāna. Jhāna is a sublime (<i>mahaggata</i>) state in which the jhāna factors (applied and sustained thought, zest, happiness, one-pointedness) are refined step by step. Calm suppresses the defilements but does not uproot them; it serves as the basis (<i>pādaka</i>) for insight.
