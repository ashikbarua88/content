question: Kamma is volition. Why does the same gift bear different results when given with or without understanding?
answer: >-
  Results follow the volition and what accompanies it: its roots, knowledge, and the clarity before and after
  the act. A gift given with understanding comes from a three-rooted citta, which can bring a three-rooted
  rebirth.

Kamma is volition (<i>cetanā</i>): “It is intention, monks, that I call kamma” (AN 6.63). The volition ceases at once, but it leaves a potency in the mental continuum that ripens, when conditions allow, as resultant consciousness (<i>vipāka</i>) and kamma-born matter. Kamma is not fate: it is one condition among many, and present volition is always open. The Saṅgaha sorts it by function, by order of ripening, by time and by plane.
