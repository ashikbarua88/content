question: The Abhidhamma counts 89 kinds of citta. What is gained by counting minds instead of describing a mind?
answer: >-
  Counting puts kinds of knowing where a single knower was assumed. Each kind is fixed by its roots, feeling,
  plane and function, so the mind can be watched in parts. And no knower is left over apart from the knowing.
---
Consciousness is the bare knowing of an object (<i>ārammaṇa-vijānana</i>); in the Dhammapada's words, mind is the forerunner (<i>pubbaṅgama</i>) of states. It never arises alone and never lasts: each citta arises, stands and dissolves in three sub-moments (<i>uppāda, ṭhiti, bhaṅga</i>), and conditions the next as it goes. The 89 kinds are sorted by plane (<i>bhūmi</i>), by kind (<i>jāti</i>: wholesome, unwholesome, resultant, functional), by roots and by feeling. Mind here is not a substance but a continuity (<i>santati</i>) of such events, with no owner behind them.
