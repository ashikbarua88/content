question: >-
  The Paṭṭhāna names twenty-four ways one thing helps another. Why would liberation need so much detail about
  conditions?
answer: >-
  Each named condition replaces a first cause or an independent agent with a relation that can be seen. And
  each one points to where change is possible: attention, association, support, repetition.
---
The Paṭṭhāna analyses every relation between dhammas into twenty-four modes: root, object, predominance, proximity, conascence, mutuality, support, decisive support, kamma, nutriment and the rest. A condition (<i>paccaya</i>) is a dhamma that helps another, the conditioned (<i>paccayuppanna</i>), to arise or to persist. The scheme puts a web of mutual support where other systems put a creator or a first cause: nothing arises from a single cause, and nothing from no cause.
