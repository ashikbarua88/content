question: Nibbāna is unconditioned and is not a citta. How can a citta take it as its object?
answer: >-
  Path and fruition cittas take Nibbāna as their object. They know it; they do not produce it. It conditions
  them as an object and as a decisive support, without itself being conditioned by anything.
---
Nibbāna is the one unconditioned element (<i>asaṅkhata-dhātu</i>). It does not arise, stand or cease, and nothing produces it; yet it serves as object for path and fruition consciousness, which know it directly. Its characteristic is peace (<i>santi</i>). It is described by negation, as the extinction of greed, hatred and delusion, and by image, as the deathless (<i>amata</i>). It is ultimate, not a concept, yet it lies outside the five aggregates and outside the three times. It is spoken of with residue (<i>sa-upādisesa</i>), while the aggregates of an arahant remain, and without residue (<i>anupādisesa</i>), when they cease.
