question: >-
  Contact, feeling, perception, volition and attention arise with every citta. What would a moment without
  volition be like, and why can there be none?
answer: >-
  Every citta has an object and gathers its factors toward it. Volition (cetanā) is that gathering, so a citta
  without it would not hold together. Even in resultant cittas volition is present; there it makes no new
  kamma.
---
Mental factors are the qualities that arise with consciousness and give it its character. Each is a dhamma in its own right, with its own characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause. They are joined to citta by four marks of association (<i>sampayutta-lakkhaṇa</i>): they arise together, cease together, share one object and one base. Fifty-two are counted: seven universals (<i>sabbacitta-sādhāraṇa</i>), six occasionals (<i>pakiṇṇaka</i>), fourteen unwholesome and twenty-five beautiful (<i>sobhana</i>). The ethical quality of a moment is decided by its factors, above all by its roots (<i>hetu</i>).
